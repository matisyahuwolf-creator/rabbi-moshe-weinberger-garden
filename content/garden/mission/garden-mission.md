# Garden Mission

## Mission

The garden exists to create kelim for Torah knowledge: not to add to what is, but to reveal the true place, relationships, divisions, and living structure of what is already there.

## Ramchal's Garden

In the introduction to [[concepts/Derech Hashem|Derech Hashem]], the [[concepts/Ramchal|Ramchal]] compares ordered knowledge to a garden arranged with flowerbeds, paths, and rows. Knowledge without its relationships and true place is not yet alive to the mind. It may contain many parts, but the parts sit like an undifferentiated thicket.

> "The perception of many parts about which we do not know their connections or true places... is nothing but a heavy and joyless burden..."

The work of this site is to move Torah learning from thicket to garden. Each source, concept, shiur, sefer, letter, and personal note should be findable in its place and understandable through its connections.

## Basi LeGani

The [[concepts/Basi LeGani|Basi LeGani]] frame deepens the same mission. Hashem's world is a garden, and the avodah is to make a dirah where the existing light can be revealed. The site is not manufacturing Torah. It is arranging kelim so the relationship between ideas can be seen, received, and lived.

## What This Means Here

- Every source should know its context.
- Every concept should know its related concepts.
- Every research area should show where its notes belong.
- Every path should clarify parts, divisions, and relationships.
- The map matters because relationship is part of the thing.
- Beauty is clarity: flowerbeds, paths, rows, and living connections.

## Practical Rules

- First ask: is this the whole thing or a part?
- Then ask: is it a category or a specific?
- Then ask: is it a cause or an effect?
- Then ask: is it the subject itself or one of its associations?
- Add links when they reveal true place, not just when two words sound related.
- Build maps, areas, and concept pages as kelim for the learning.
- Do not add content merely to add content.
